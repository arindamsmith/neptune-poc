# Best-in-Class Text Extraction for Medical PDFs in LTD Claims Processing: A 2026 Decision Report

## TL;DR
- **Replace raw Textract-only OCR with a vision-LLM-centric hybrid, but keep Textract as a complementary layer.** The highest-accuracy, still-scalable-and-HIPAA-compliant approach for your hard inputs (handwritten clinical notes, low-quality scans, complex tables) is a **tiered hybrid: Amazon Bedrock Data Automation (BDA) and/or a Claude vision model on Amazon Bedrock for handwriting/tables + Textract for clean printed/forms pages**, with clean, reading-order text then fed to Comprehend Medical. This keeps everything under one AWS Business Associate Agreement (BAA) and inside your existing S3/Lambda/Step Functions stack.
- **Vision LLMs materially beat Textract on your exact failure modes.** In the OmniAI OCR benchmark (Feb 2025), Textract scored 74% overall JSON accuracy vs. 86% for Gemini 2.0 Flash and 85% for Azure Document Intelligence; on line-item/table extraction, GPT-4o direct-image scored 90.5% vs Textract's 82% (BusinessWareTech). LLMs' contextual reasoning is precisely what fixes the "rotator cuff → rotator wiff" and "4-April → 4-Ail" substitution errors, because the model uses medical and date context that character-level OCR lacks.
- **The single cheapest immediate win is LLM post-OCR correction** of your existing Textract output (research shows 40–60%+ reductions in character error rate), but full vision-model re-OCR of the hardest pages is still required to fix table scrambling, signatures, and handwriting that Textract never captured correctly in the first place.

## Key Findings

### 1. Textract is at/near its ceiling on your hardest inputs; tuning helps but won't close the gap
Textract's own documentation and multiple independent benchmarks confirm it is strong on clean printed forms/tables but degrades on handwriting, low-quality scans, dense/merged tables, and non-standard layouts. AWS's own guidance states handwriting is English-only, images should be at least 150 DPI (300 DPI is the broader OCR best practice), and tables must be visually well-separated to extract reliably. The June 2025 Textract accuracy update added support for superscripts/subscripts/rotated text and improved low-resolution/fax handling and confusable characters (e.g., 0 vs O) — but it does not fundamentally solve cursive clinical handwriting or table-order scrambling. Image preprocessing (300 DPI, deskew, binarize, denoise, contrast enhancement) is documented to lift OCR accuracy by roughly 10–30% and should be done regardless — but this is incremental, not transformative. Textract's Custom Queries feature can improve field-level extraction on known fields but does not retrain the underlying model.

### 2. Vision LLMs are the current accuracy leaders on handwriting, medical terms, and tables
- **OmniAI OCR Benchmark (getomni.ai, Feb 2025, 1,000 documents, structured JSON extraction):** Gemini 2.0 Flash 86%, Azure Document Intelligence 85%, GPT-4o 75%, AWS Textract 74%, Anthropic Claude 3.5 Sonnet 69%, Google Document AI 68% (OmniAI's own product scored 92%; Unstructured 51%). The benchmark explicitly notes VLMs' "most notable performance gains were in documents with charts/infographics, handwriting, or complex input fields." Note this benchmark has not been updated since Feb 2025, so newer Gemini/Claude/GPT versions likely score higher than these figures; AIMultiple's Nov 2025 benchmark now ranks GPT-5, olmOCR-2-7B, and Gemini 2.5/3 Pro highest on handwriting.
- **Handwriting (academic HTR):** Crosilla, Klic & Colavizza (arXiv:2503.15195, Mar 2025) found GPT-4o-mini attains 1.71% CER / 3.34% WER on the IAM dataset, while GPT-4o reaches 1.69% CER / 3.66% WER on RIMES — outperforming Transkribus' specialized "supermodel" and approaching human transcription.
- **Medical handwritten forms (the closest match to your use case):** "From Handwriting to Structured Data" (arXiv:2604.16504, Apr 2026), which benchmarked 17 models on a redacted 49-document set of real handwritten medical case-record forms, found "the latest Google and OpenAI models reach accuracies around 85% with weighted F1 scores ≃90%" on discrete fields; "GPT 5.4 excels in noisy date extraction as well as reliability with the lowest hallucination rate (6%)," while "Gemini 3.1 delivered the best overall performance, with the lowest free text error rates (WER = 0.50 and CER = 0.31)."
- **Tables/line items:** GPT-4o direct-image extraction scored 90.5% vs Textract's 82% on line-item extraction (BusinessWareTech, via Parsli 2026). Specialized open-source VLMs are even stronger on tables specifically: dots.ocr (a 1.7B-parameter open model, arXiv:2512.02498) achieves 88.6% TableTEDS vs GPT-5.4's 72.0% (CodeSOTA/OmniDocBench).
- **Caveat — hallucination:** Unlike Textract (which mis-reads), generative models can *invent* plausible-but-wrong content. Every medical benchmark flags non-trivial hallucination rates (~6–7% even on the best models). This is a genuine patient-safety and claims-integrity risk that requires confidence scoring, visual grounding (bounding boxes), and human-in-the-loop review.

### 3. Comprehend Medical accuracy is bottlenecked by input text quality — fix the OCR and coding improves automatically
Amazon Comprehend Medical (HIPAA-eligible; ICD-10-CM, RxNorm, and SNOMED CT ontology linking; priced per 100-character unit) is context-sensitive: garbled input like "rotator wiff" or "4-Ail" directly causes missed entities and failed code mappings. AWS's own medical-coding guidance notes the pipeline is "more sensitive to missing entities than to redundant ones" — i.e., dropped or garbled text kills claims, because an unstated diagnosis fails to trigger billing rules and can cause claim rejection. Cleaner upstream text is therefore the highest-leverage fix for downstream coding accuracy.

### 4. All major options are HIPAA-eligible under a BAA, but the AWS path minimizes contractual and architectural friction
- **Amazon Bedrock (incl. Bedrock Data Automation) + Textract + Comprehend Medical:** all HIPAA-eligible; a single AWS BAA (self-service via AWS Artifact) covers all of them, including Claude models accessed *through* Bedrock. Bedrock does not train on your data, supports VPC PrivateLink, KMS encryption, and CloudTrail logging. (Bedrock and Bedrock AgentCore were confirmed HIPAA-eligible as of early 2026.)
- **Azure AI Document Intelligence + Azure OpenAI:** HIPAA/BAA covered (BAA auto-included via Microsoft Product Terms + DPA, downloadable from the Service Trust Portal); strong printed and handwriting accuracy (99%+ claimed on prebuilt models); offers Docker container/on-prem deployment for air-gapped processing.
- **Google Document AI + Gemini (Vertex AI) + Healthcare NLP:** HIPAA-eligible under Google's BAA; healthcare-specific processors (CDA and FHIR parsers), and a Form Parser demonstrated on handwritten medical intake forms; MedGemma available for medical document understanding.
- **Anthropic Claude direct API:** BAA available on Enterprise/API HIPAA-ready tiers — but if you access Claude via Bedrock, the AWS BAA governs (not Anthropic's), which is simpler for an AWS shop. Anthropic's own BAA does not apply to Claude purchased through a third-party cloud.
- **John Snow Labs (Healthcare NLP + Visual NLP/Spark OCR):** self-hosted/in-VPC (data never leaves your environment); its Healthcare NLP 6.2.0 release notes claim "92% accuracy on handwritten medical documents with Vision Language Models" plus "zero data leaving your environment with HIPAA, GDPR, SOC 2, and HITRUST compliance," and purpose-built medical NER/entity resolution (RxNorm, ICD-10, SNOMED) that can rival or replace Comprehend Medical; deployable via AWS Marketplace.

### 5. Cost: LLMs are pricier per page than Textract OCR, but not prohibitively so — and routing controls the bill
- **Textract:** DetectDocumentText $1.50/1,000 pages; Tables $15/1,000; Forms $50/1,000; Forms+Tables ~$65/1,000; Queries $15/1,000. Layout and Signatures are free when combined with paid features. Volume discounts apply above 1M pages/month.
- **Bedrock Data Automation:** ~$0.010/page for standard output; ~$0.040/page for custom-blueprint output (+$0.0005/field beyond 30 fields). Flat per-page, managed; includes table/figure/layout extraction plus confidence scores, visual grounding, and built-in hallucination mitigation.
- **Vision LLM (token-based):** Claude Sonnet on Bedrock ~$3/$15 per million input/output tokens (batch mode 50% off). A typical page is a few cents; whole-document VLM OCR is materially pricier than plain Textract OCR but competitive with Textract Forms+Tables. Gemini Flash 2.0-class models can process ~6,000 pages for ~$1 (Vellum, via Parsli).
- **Comprehend Medical:** billed per 100-character unit (NERe), with ontology linking billed higher; first-month free tier of 8.5M characters.
- **Azure Document Intelligence:** Read $1.50/1,000; Prebuilt $10/1,000; Custom $30–50/1,000; commitment tiers drop to ~$0.53–0.60/1,000 at high (8M+/month) volume.
- **Google Document AI:** Enterprise OCR ~$1.50/1,000; Form Parser $30/1,000.

### Narrowed Top 3
1. **AWS-native tiered hybrid — Bedrock Data Automation + Claude vision (hardest pages) + Textract (easy pages) → Comprehend Medical.** Best balance of accuracy, scale, cost control, single-BAA compliance, and zero cross-cloud PHI egress. **(Recommended.)**
2. **Textract + Bedrock LLM post-OCR correction.** Fastest, cheapest lift; the right first step and a viable long-term option if validation shows it closes most of the gap.
3. **John Snow Labs Healthcare NLP + Visual NLP (in-VPC).** Best if you need maximum medical specialization, want data to never leave your environment, or want to consolidate OCR + medical NER in one purpose-built stack.

### Downstream integration note
Whatever the front-end OCR, the pattern is the same: **OCR/VLM → clean, reading-order text (Markdown/HTML for tables) → Comprehend Medical (NERe + ICD-10-CM / RxNorm / SNOMED CT) → business rules → human review of low-confidence items.** Preserving table structure as Markdown/HTML before Comprehend Medical materially improves entity and relationship extraction versus flattened text, and directly addresses your "scrambled table sequence" problem.

## Recommendations

### Recommended best approach: A tiered, AWS-native hybrid built on Bedrock Data Automation + Claude vision, with Textract retained for easy pages

**Why this wins for your specific LTD use case:** it maximizes accuracy on exactly your documented failure modes (cursive handwriting, complex tables, clinical-term and date substitution) while remaining scalable, cost-controlled via routing, and entirely under one AWS BAA inside your existing S3/Lambda/Step Functions/Comprehend Medical pipeline. It avoids cross-cloud PHI egress and dual-BAA complexity, and it mirrors AWS's own published medical-record-digitization reference architecture (BDA → HealthLake FHIR) and its documented Textract-vs-BDA cost-optimized hybrid pattern.

**Staged rollout:**

1. **Stage 0 — Immediate, low-risk (weeks): preprocess + LLM post-OCR correction.** Add image preprocessing (300 DPI, deskew, binarize, denoise, contrast) before Textract, and add a Claude-on-Bedrock post-OCR correction pass prompted to fix clinical terminology and dates *without inventing content* (low temperature; instruction: "only correct obvious OCR errors, never add facts"). Expect a substantial reduction in substitution errors feeding Comprehend Medical. Validate against a labeled ground-truth set of your real documents.
   - *Threshold to escalate:* if post-correction still misses handwriting, signatures, or scrambles tables (it will, because the underlying Textract capture failed), proceed to Stage 1.

2. **Stage 1 — Page routing + Bedrock Data Automation.** Use a cheap Bedrock classification call to route each page: clean printed/standard forms → Textract (cheapest); handwritten/low-quality/complex-table pages → **BDA** (~$0.01–0.04/page, with confidence scores + visual grounding) or a **Claude vision** pass for the very hardest. Normalize all outputs to reading-order Markdown. Classification is fractions of a cent and routing a standard page to Textract instead of an LLM can cut per-document cost by ~75%.

3. **Stage 2 — Feed clean text to Comprehend Medical + human-in-the-loop.** Route Comprehend Medical low-confidence entities and any low-confidence OCR to Amazon A2I human review. Use confidence thresholds of ≥90–95% for anything affecting a claim decision (AWS best practice for financial-decision workflows). Retain visual grounding/bounding boxes so reviewers can verify against the source.

4. **Stage 3 — Optimize cost.** Move batchable volume to Bedrock batch mode (50% off) and Textract async; reserve whole-page VLM OCR for pages that truly need it. Track per-feature Textract spend via CloudTrail, since Forms+Tables at ~$65/1,000 dominates the bill.

**Accuracy-vs-cost tradeoffs (explicit):**
- *Cheapest, smallest lift:* preprocessing + LLM post-correction of Textract (Stage 0).
- *Best accuracy per dollar for the hard tail:* BDA for handwriting/tables (~$0.01–0.04/page, managed).
- *Maximum accuracy, highest cost:* whole-page Claude/Gemini VLM OCR on every page — reserve for the hardest pages only.
- *If you must eliminate cloud PHI egress or want the strongest medical-specific NER:* evaluate **John Snow Labs in-VPC** as a replacement for both the VLM OCR and Comprehend Medical.

### Benchmarks/thresholds that would change the recommendation
- If, on YOUR labeled document set, BDA/Claude do not beat tuned Textract by a meaningful margin (e.g., <5 percentage points), stay on tuned Textract + post-correction (cheaper).
- If hallucination on medical entities exceeds your tolerance during validation, prefer extractive Textract/BDA output with visual grounding over free-form VLM transcription, and expand human review.
- If procurement mandates zero cloud PHI processing, pivot to self-hosted John Snow Labs or open-source VLMs (dots.ocr, Qwen2.5-VL, PaddleOCR-VL) in your VPC.

## Caveats
- **Fast-moving area:** model names and prices change monthly (GPT-5.x, Gemini 3.x, Claude Opus 4.x all appear in 2025–2026 sources). Re-benchmark before committing; treat vendor-reported accuracy (John Snow Labs' 92%, Azure's "99%+") as directional, not independently audited.
- **Benchmarks ≠ your documents:** OmniAI, AIMultiple, and academic sets use general or non-US-insurance documents. Your LTD medical mix (faxed notes, cursive, checkboxes, multi-column tables) must be validated on a labeled internal ground-truth set — build one before finalizing vendor choice and confidence thresholds.
- **Hallucination is the new failure mode:** generative OCR can produce fluent-but-wrong text, which is more dangerous in claims than an obvious garble. Mandate confidence thresholds, visual grounding, and human review for anything affecting a claim decision.
- **HIPAA-eligible ≠ compliant:** a signed BAA plus correct architecture (VPC endpoints, KMS, IAM least-privilege, CloudTrail logging, minimizing PHI in prompts) is required; eligibility alone is not compliance. Note that Comprehend Medical DetectPHI can also serve as a de-identification/tokenization layer before any generative model call if you choose to minimize PHI exposure.
- **OmniAI benchmark limitations:** it uses GPT-4o as its judge, includes OmniAI's own product (which scored highest — a potential bias), measures structured JSON extraction rather than raw text, and has not been updated since Feb 2025, so current frontier models likely outperform the cited figures.
